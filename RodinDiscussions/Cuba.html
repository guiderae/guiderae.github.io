Cuba

Discussed November 5, 2026

Our November Rodin Group gathering was hosted by Larry Chamberlain and delt with “Cuba…our island neighbor. A non-democratic/communist stain in the Western Hemisphere since the beginning of the 19th century.” Ahead of the gathering, Larry asked us to focus on 5 key questions:

1. Why is the US so opposed to Cuba?2. Why don’t we just annex the beautiful island and “sell it to Marriott or Hilton? 😂🤣😂

3. What influences exist in the US that steer our reactions to the Cuban government?4. Why hasn’t the current approach taken by the US worked?5. What strategy(s) would you employ if you were President of the USA?

The Facts: US/Cuba KEY Historical Bullets:

1. 1898 - US intervened in the Cuban War of independence leading to the Spanish-American

War. Spain was defeated and ceded Cuba, Puerto Rico, and the Philippines.

2. 1902 – Republic of Cuba is established, and the US ends direct military occupation. Platt

Amendment allows the US to intervene militarily in Cuban affairs and leases land for a naval base at Guantanamo Bay.

3. 1909 – After Cuban domestic rebellion US re-occupies Cuba.4. 1959/1960 – Castro overthrows Batista’s regime and Cuba nationalizes all US owned

businesses and sugar mills without any compensation, resulting in President Eisenhower imposing a partial trade embargo.

5. 1961/1962 – Bay of Pigs invasion fails. President Kennedy imposes near-total embargo, and

the Cuban Missile crisis erupts with a 13-day naval block aid stopping Russia from building and removing their missiles from Cuba.

6. 2014 – President Obama and Raul Castro restore diplomatic ties.7. 2020/2021 - President Trump cancels US flights to Cuba and declares them a sponsor of

terrorism before leaving office.

8. 2022/2025 - President Biden reinstates flights to and from Cuba, reinstates full processing of

immigrant visas, and expanded US/Cuban trade. Additionally, 74,000 Cubans travel to the US under the humanitarian parole program and removes Cuba from the list of state sponsors of terrorism.

9. 2025/2026 – President Trump revokes temporary legal status, increases tariffs, reinstates no

transfer of money from the US to Cuba, and creates a total embargo of Cuba.

OUR DISCUSSION:We reviewed Larry’s questions as well as the history of the US and Cuba as stated above and began talking about the good/bad/ugly of the current political relationship. The points that were discussed included:

1. The massive decline of the population of Cuba with an estimate of between 25 to 30% of the

people exiting to other countries since 2020. Translated into actual numbers the percentage has gone from 10 million Cuban residents to approximately 7 to 7.5 million.

2. The significant decline of the general economy. For example, the cost of a dozen eggs costing

a week’s salary of those who are currently employed.

3. It was pointed out that not only was there a free fall of residents but:

a. the significant shortages of fuel and gas b. nearly no tourism c. most of the power grid of Cuba was failing because of age and the lack of parts for

repair translating to almost no electricity throughout the country.

4. In the past, one of the strengths of Cuba was its medical training and healthcare. With the

economic demise, lack of electrical power in the hospitals, lack of medical parts to repair equipment made in the US, there has been a mass exodus of doctors and skilled workers. This departure from Cuba (which had an extraordinarily high literacy rate of doctors) has resulted in a major loss of revenue to the country.

5. A recent survey of 1000+ Cuban Americans in South Florida, showed that over 70% still favor

maximum pressure, oil blockades and even US military action to overthrow the Cuban government. It was noted that even though the older generations (who lost businesses and land} still feel strongly about overthrowing the government, the younger generations feel that the US should build a stronger open relationship with the island. Additionally, the younger generation also highlighted the fact that over 30% of the

Afro-Cubans suffer from higher poverty and police violence and should not discriminated against.OUR CONCLUSIONS:Larry’s final question was: “what would we do if we were the President of the US”?

1. There was broad agreement that the embargo should be lifted entirely since the Cuban

government is using the US as a scapegoat and hiding their lack of accountability in all the failures Cuba is experiencing. The embargo is also creating an opening for Russia and China to strengthen their ties throughout the western hemisphere.

2. Reopening embassies and a diplomacy shift would change the US’s posture from aggressor to

re-establishing diplomatic and business relationship. It would also allow the US to begin to establish ground rules against nationalization. By taking this approach, it would incentivize US private companies to go in and hire/train local workers to inject economic growth into the lower classes. It would also allow for the rebuilding of infrastructure to create more job opportunities.

3. Because of this positive shift, we felt that the autocratic Cuban government would reduce ties

to Russia and China, soften their control of the shrinking population, and hopefully incentivize people to return and give them stronger leverage in running the country.

4. By doing all this it would also renew the cultural connection with the US and regenerate

baseball to decrease tension between us and Cuba.
